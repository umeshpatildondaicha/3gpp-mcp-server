What the divergences actually mean

  • The 5 "SCOPE-GATED" rows are not a routing loss — they're a corpus gap our gate correctly surfaces. PFCP (29.244), IMS SIP (24.229), charging (32.240/298), GTP-U (29.281) aren't in our index, so our gate abstains and points to WebSearch instead of returning a confident near-miss. TejVox indexes those core specs, so he answers. Same routing logic would answer too — if we ingested the specs (the markers auto-disable when the spec appears). So this is a coverage decision, and abstaining beats a wrong high-confidence answer.
  • The 1 "miss" (row 19) is the real routing weakness. "walk me through how the UE comes back after losing the cell" has no procedure keyword, so our lexical classifier falls through to CLAUSE_LOOKUP; a model classifier reads the intent and routes to PROCEDURE. This is the price of the deterministic approach.
  • On the 14 keyword-bearing queries we route identically — procedure, clause-lookup, troubleshooting, and vendor/CVE out-of-scope all land where his stage would put them, at zero model cost and fully reproducibly (unit-tested).
  • Our scope gate is the more principled OOS mechanism. It fires on knowledge (owning spec absent), not confidence — and on this corpus the confidence signal is inverted (absent-owner questions score higher), so a confidence/model gate is exposed to exactly the case ours catches.

Verdict

On routing logic, you're at parity for anything with a trigger keyword, and ahead on out-of-scope rigor. TejVox leads on two things this probe exposes: (1) recall on keyword-less paraphrases (model beats lexical), and (2) corpus breadth — his core-spec coverage means he answers where you (correctly) abstain. Closing (1) is a cheap paraphrase fallback; closing (2) is ingesting 29.244 / 24.229 / 32.240 / 32.298 / 29.281, after which your gate stops firing on them automatically.
